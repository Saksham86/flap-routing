We study whether choosing an LLM's compression level per query pays off on edge hardware. We port FLAP, a retraining-free structured pruning method, to SmolLM2-1.7B and obtain a ladder of nested pruned variants of one model, stored as small pruning plans over a single copy of the weights. We build a 19{,}242-question pool from eight benchmarks with reproducible splits and a scoring pipeline that checks every result against the evaluation harness. On a Tesla T4, FLAP raises WikiText-2 perplexity from 18.48 to 25.94 at 20\% and 124.62 at 50\% of prunable parameters removed (Wanda-SP: 549.25 at 50\%; Magnitude-SP fails), reproducing the FLAP paper's method ranking at this scale. Pruning 50\% cuts 1024-token prefill latency by 38\% and energy per generated token by 27\%, but batch-1 decode latency stays at 30.4--31.1\,ms per token for every ratio. Per-variant accuracy on the query pool, the oracle headroom available to a router, and laptop-CPU latencies are being measured and are reported as pending.
