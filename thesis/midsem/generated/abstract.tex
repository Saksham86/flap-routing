We study whether choosing an LLM's compression level per query pays off on edge hardware. We port FLAP, a retraining-free structured pruning method, to SmolLM2-1.7B and obtain a ladder of nested pruned variants of one model, stored as small pruning plans over a single copy of the weights. We build a 19{,}242-question pool from eight benchmarks with reproducible splits and a scoring pipeline that checks every result against the evaluation harness. On a Tesla T4, FLAP raises WikiText-2 perplexity from 18.48 to 25.94 at 20\% and 124.62 at 50\% of prunable parameters removed (Wanda-SP: 549.25 at 50\%; Magnitude-SP fails), reproducing the FLAP paper's method ranking at this scale. Pruning 50\% cuts 1024-token prefill latency by 38\% and energy per generated token by 27\%, but batch-1 decode latency stays at 30.4--31.1\,ms per token for every ratio. On 3{,}849 test questions, macro accuracy falls from 62.5\% (unpruned) to 47.3\% (FLAP 20\%), 40.0\% (FLAP 35\%), 32.6\% (FLAP 50\%); several pruned variants collapse to a constant answer on MMLU and BoolQ and fail to finish GSM8K answers. A per-query oracle over the variants reaches 75.8\% versus 64.1\% for the best fixed variant, but once variants that do not beat a constant answer are excluded the gain shrinks to 1.9 points (66.1\%), so on this GPU the headroom for per-query compression routing is small. Laptop-CPU latencies are pending.
