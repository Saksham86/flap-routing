\subsection{Measurement infrastructure (P0)}
On the Colab Tesla T4 all environment checks passed: seeding reproduced identical tensors; a model whose layers have different numbers of heads and MLP channels plus compensation biases (the shape FLAP produces) gave the same logits whether pruned physically or by masking, in fp32 and fp16 and with eager and SDPA attention (maximum fp16 logit difference 0.0). The NVML energy counter is supported on the T4 and agrees with the independent 20\,ms power sampler to within 1.7\% at idle (30.2\,J vs.\ 30.7\,J over 3.0\,s, mean 10.2\,W) and 1.1\% under an fp16 matrix-multiply load (692.4\,J vs.\ 700.0\,J over 10.0\,s, mean 69.7\,W, 23.6\,TFLOPS). Energy figures in this work therefore use the counter.

\paragraph{Analysis.} \emph{Quality.} At every ratio tested (20\%, 35\%, 50\%) the ranking FLAP $<$ Wanda-SP $<$ Magnitude-SP in perplexity holds, as reported for LLaMA in the FLAP paper. FLAP's perplexity rises from 18.48 (unpruned) to 25.94 at 20\% ($\times$1.40), 51.12 at 35\% ($\times$2.77) and 124.62 at 50\% ($\times$6.74); Wanda-SP reaches 549.25 at 50\%, and Magnitude-SP is unusable at every ratio ($5.3\times10^{6}$ already at 20\%). FLAP's adaptive structure is visible in the plans: at 50\% one layer keeps only 4 of its 32 heads, while the uniform baselines keep 16 in every layer. \emph{Latency and energy on the T4.} Decode latency is essentially independent of the pruning ratio: 30.4--31.1\,ms per token from 0\% to 60\% (all individual 64-token generations took 1.89--2.19\,s), so width pruning does not shorten generation here. This is consistent with batch-1 decoding of a 1.7\,B model on this GPU being dominated by fixed per-step overheads rather than by the size of the weight matrices; the cause was not measured directly. Prefill does become faster as prompts get longer: at 50\% the 1024-token prefill drops from 227.7 to 141.7\,ms ($-$38\%), the 512-token prefill from 114.9 to 74.6\,ms ($-$35\%), while the 128-token prefill barely changes (32.9 vs.\ 28.7\,ms). Energy per generated token falls from 2.09 to 1.52\,J ($-$27\%) at 50\% because the mean GPU power during generation drops from 67.9 to 48.9\,W at the same duration; peak GPU memory falls from 6.48 to 5.00\,GB. The latency benefit of width pruning on this GPU is therefore limited to prefill; the energy and memory benefits are measurable. The laptop CPU is measured separately.

\paragraph{Oracle headroom.} The plain oracle (Eq.~\eqref{eq:headroom}) counts a question as solved if any variant answers it correctly. When some variants answer every question identically, combining them ``solves'' questions that no router could identify (two variants that always answer ``yes'' and always answer ``no'' together cover all of BoolQ). We therefore also report an \emph{informative oracle}: on each task a variant may be chosen only if its accuracy beats the best constant answer (one-sided binomial test, $p<0.01$). Table~\ref{tab:headroom} gives both, with the accuracy expected if the variants' errors were independent, $1-\prod_v(1-A_v)$. \subsection{Query-level confidence cascade (offline)}
A cheap variant answers each multiple-choice question first; its answer is kept if its confidence (the probability of its chosen option) is at least $\tau$, otherwise the question is escalated to the unpruned model. The test questions are split into two fixed halves: $\tau$ is chosen on one half (largest share served within an accuracy-loss budget) and all numbers are reported on the other half. Escalated questions pay for both models, so the cascade is cheaper than the unpruned model only if the share served exceeds the cheap model's cost ratio.

The pruned variants' confidence is informative --- at a 0.5-point budget FLAP 20\% answers 19.8\% of the questions itself --- but every configuration costs more than the unpruned model (1.86--2.03$\times$ on the T4), because the share served stays far below the cost ratio of the cheap variant. A query-level cascade between these FLAP variants cannot save cost; the planned fix is a token-level cascade (Section~\ref{sec:plan}).
