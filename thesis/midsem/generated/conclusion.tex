The infrastructure for the study is in place and verified: a seeded, config-driven pipeline with recorded provenance, a faithful and tested port of FLAP and two baselines, a reproducible 19{,}242-question pool, and a scoring pipeline whose every result is cross-checked against lm-eval. The first measurements answer RQ1: FLAP produces a steep accuracy--cost ladder on a 1.7\,B model (perplexity $\times$1.40 at 20\%, $\times$6.74 at 50\%) and keeps its advantage over Wanda-SP and Magnitude-SP. They also show that on a T4 at batch size 1 width pruning reduces prefill latency, energy and memory but not decode latency, so the cost model used for routing must be measured per device rather than assumed proportional to parameter count. For RQ2, the outcome matrix shows that the plain oracle overstates what routing can gain: 75.8\% against 64.1\% for the best fixed variant, but much of that gain comes from pruned variants that answer every question identically. Restricted to variants that beat a constant answer, the oracle reaches 66.1\% and still sends 95.7\% of questions to the unpruned model. On the T4, where all variants cost about the same per multiple-choice question and the pruned variants generate longer GSM8K answers, per-query compression routing therefore has little to gain. The planned next steps are to measure whether the laptop CPU, where pruning does shorten each step, changes this, and to test less aggressive ratios (10--20\%) where the pruned variants still beat a constant answer on more tasks.
